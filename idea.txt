Kill Factors: 

Gate 1: Vocabulary Stability (The Distance Ratio)
Metric: d_OOD / D where d_OOD is the mean Euclidean distance of an OOD key vector to its nearest Wikipedia centroid, and D is the mean pairwise distance between the Wikipedia centroids themselves.
The In-Domain Anchor: Compute d_val / D on the WikiText validation split first to find the structural floor of the model's natural quantization noise.
Kill Criterion: d_code / D > 0.40 OR d_code / d_val > 1.5. If the code domain forces vectors to sit nearly halfway between cluster centers, or causes a 50% expansion over the in-domain baseline, the vocabulary does not generalize.

Gate 2: Dynamics Stability (Subspace & Distribution Drift)
Metric A (Topology): Maximum Principal Angle theta_max between the subspace spanned by the top-4 eigenvectors of L_wiki and the top-4 eigenvectors of L_OOD.
Kill Criterion A: theta_max > 35 degrees. If the community boundaries rotate past this point, the offline structural partitions will slice directly through the middle of live workloads.
Metric B (Transitions): Mean Kullback-Leibler Divergence across all rows of the transition matrices: (1 / |V|) * sum_i( D_KL( P_wiki^(i) || P_OOD^(i) ) ).
Kill Criterion B: Mean D_KL > 0.70 bits. Beyond this threshold, the predictive ranking of successor clusters scrambles completely, turning the Markov prefetcher into random guesswork.

Gate 3: Functional Baseline (The LRU Margin)
Metric: Absolute difference in token-level KV cache hit rate at an identical fixed cache capacity constraint C (e.g., C = 32 or 64 clusters).
Kill Criterion: HR_GSP(C) - HR_LRU(C) < 4.0% (Absolute). If the GSP policy cannot outperform a standard, zero-overhead sliding-window LRU cache by at least 4 absolute percentage points on the in-domain or mid-OOD validation sets, the hardware runtime complexity is unjustified.
